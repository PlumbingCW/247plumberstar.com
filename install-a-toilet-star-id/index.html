How to Install a Toilet in Star: Flange, Wax Ring, and Bolts

To install a toilet, shut off the water, drain and remove the old toilet, and scrape off the old wax. Check that the closet flange is solid and level with the finished floor. Then set a new wax ring, lower the bowl straight down onto the flange bolts, tighten the nuts a little at a time, and connect the water to test. Bring a helper for the lifting. The flange is where most installs go wrong.

The steps below cover each stage, with the checks that prevent leaks. If the flange is broken, the floor is soft, or water has been seeping, stop and call our team at (208) 679-8357. Our toilet repair plumbers handle those problems every week.

Before you buy: measure and gather

Measure from the wall behind the toilet to the center of the flange bolts. Most are 12 inches, but 10 and 14 inch rough-ins exist. A toilet built for 12 will not fit a 10 inch rough-in.

The installation, step by step

1

Shut off, flush, and drain

Close the stop valve, flush, and sponge out the tank and bowl. Disconnect the supply line.

2

Unbolt and lift the old toilet

Remove the nut caps and nuts, rock the bowl loose, and lift straight up. It is heavy, so ask for help. Plug the drain opening with a rag to block sewer gas.

3

Scrape the old wax and inspect the flange

Remove every bit of old wax. The flange should be solid, not cracked, and sit level with or slightly above the finished floor.

4

Set the new wax ring and bolts

Put the new bolts in the flange slots, then press the wax ring onto the flange or the bowl horn, not both.

5

Lower the bowl and press down

Line the bowl up with the bolts, lower it straight down, and press with your weight. Do not twist or rock it afterward.

6

Tighten, connect, and test

Tighten the nuts evenly, a half turn at a time, until the bowl is snug. Attach the tank if separate, connect water, flush several times, and look for drips.

The flange: where most installs fail

The flange is the ring on the floor that connects the toilet to the drain. If it sits below the floor, a standard wax ring may not seal, and an extra-thick ring or a flange spacer is needed. If it is cracked or rotted, a repair ring or a new flange is the fix.

Flange too low

A flange spacer or thicker seal raises the seat. Do not stack two wax rings and hope.

Flange cracked

A repair ring screws over the old one if the floor beneath is solid.

Floor soft or stained

Stop. Old leaks may have rotted the subfloor. Fixing the floor comes first.

A soft floor can also mean a slow leak you have not seen yet. Our leak detection team can trace one before you build on a damaged subfloor.

Permits and ownership rules in Star

The Idaho Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses says a permit is required when any plumbing work is performed. Homeowners may buy a permit only for plumbing on their own residence and related outbuildings, never for commercial buildings. Unlicensed plumbing work carries civil penalties.

What that means: before you swap a toilet in a rental or commercial space in Star, check who may legally do the work. For your own home, call Star City Hall at (208) 286-7247 or DOPL and ask if a like-for-like toilet swap needs a permit. Do that before you start, not after.

Source type: Idaho DOPL

Homeowners in Middleton and Eagle should ask their own city building department the same question, because local handling varies.
