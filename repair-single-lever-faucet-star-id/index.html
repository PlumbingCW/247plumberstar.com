How to Repair a Single-Lever Faucet in Star, ID

Most single-lever faucets that drip, leak at the base, or feel stiff need a new cartridge, the cylinder inside the body that controls flow and temperature. Shut off the water, pop the handle, pull the retaining clip or nut, lift out the old cartridge, drop in a matching one, and reassemble. The job takes about half an hour for a first-timer and needs only a few hand tools.

Below are the tools, the steps, how to match the part, and the signs that the faucet itself is worn out. If you hit a snag, our plumbers are a call away at (208) 679-8357.

Which faucet part is failing

A single-lever faucet has few parts, so the symptom points to the cause. Drips from the spout mean the cartridge or its seals. Water around the base of the handle usually means an O-ring. A hot or cold mix that wanders or a stiff lever points to mineral buildup in the cartridge.

Drip from the spoutCartridge or seat sealsReplace the cartridge

Gather these before you open the faucet

Tools and parts

  • ✓A replacement cartridge that matches your faucet, or the brand's repair kit
  • ✓Allen wrenches in small sizes and a small flat screwdriver
  • ✓Adjustable pliers and a cloth to protect the finish
  • ✓Plumber's grease for the O-rings
  • ✓A towel or plug for the drain so no small parts fall in
  • ✓White vinegar and an old toothbrush for cleaning the body

Take the old cartridge to the store, or note the faucet brand and model from the body or under the sink. Cartridges that look alike can differ in length, stem splines, and flow slots, and a near-match will leak.

Replacing the cartridge, step by step

1

Shut off the water

Close the hot and cold shutoff valves under the sink. Open the faucet to drain the lines and relieve pressure. Plug the drain.

2

Remove the handle

Look for a set screw under a cap or at the back of the lever. Loosen it with the right Allen wrench and lift the handle off.

3

Pull the cartridge

Unscrew the cap or dome. Remove the retaining clip or nut. Grip the cartridge stem and pull straight up, rocking it slightly if it sticks.

4

Clean, install, and reassemble

Wipe the cavity and spray the seats clean. Lubricate the new O-rings, seat the new cartridge in the same orientation, and reverse the steps.

5

Test

Open the lever, then turn the shutoffs on slowly. Check hot and cold, flow, and leaks around the base.

Note the orientation

Most cartridges only fit one way. Before pulling the old one, snap a photo of how the notch, tab, or arrow lines up. A cartridge installed backwards makes hot water come out on the cold side.

When a new cartridge will not fix it

If the body is pitted, cracked, or the seat inside is worn, a new cartridge will drip again. A faucet that has been repaired more than once, or that has corroded around the base, is usually cheaper to replace than to chase. Our plumbers handle both repairs and replacements on our faucet repair service.

Stop and call if the shutoff valve will not close, because forcing it can snap the stem and flood the cabinet. If the supply lines look corroded or the cabinet floor is stained, the faucet may be only one of the problems. A slow, hidden leak under a sink is the type leak detection catches before the cabinet rots.
