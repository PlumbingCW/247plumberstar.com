Sewer clog guide

Sewer Line Clog Repair in Star, ID: Causes, First Steps, and Fixes

A sewer line clog usually shows itself when several drains slow down together, a toilet gurgles when the washer runs, or the lowest drain in the house backs up. Stop using water, keep the washer and dishwasher off, and call a plumber. The usual repair is a cable or a jetter run through the line, and a camera shows why it clogged so it does not return.

This guide explains the causes, what to do while you wait, how the repair works, and who is responsible for which stretch of pipe. If sewage is already coming up, call our 24/7 line at (208) 679-8357.

What to do right now

Until the plumber arrives

  • ✓Stop running water in the whole house, including the washer, dishwasher, and showers
  • ✓Do not flush toilets once a tub or shower drain is gurgling or filling
  • ✓Move rugs and stored items off the floor around the low drain
  • ✓Avoid chemical openers, which sit in a blocked line and are hazardous to the plumber
  • ✓Find the cleanout, a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard, and keep it clear for access

If sewage is on the floor, keep kids and pets away and wash your hands well after cleanup. Open the cleanout cap only if a plumber asks you to, since a blocked line can push water out of it.

Telling a main line clog from a fixture clog

Where the symptom shows up and what it means
SymptomLikely locationUrgency
One sink drains slowlyThat fixture's trap or branchLow, plunge or cable
Tub gurgles when the toilet flushesShared branch lineModerate
Several fixtures slow at onceMain line or a large branchHigh
Lowest drain or floor drain backs upMain sewer lineCall right away
Sewage smell outside near the cleanoutMain line or lateralCall, avoid the area

The lowest opening in the house shows trouble first because wastewater rises there before anywhere else. A basement floor drain or a ground-level shower is often the first to answer.

What causes a sewer line to clog

Tree roots

Roots find the damp seam of a joint and grow inside the pipe. They catch paper and grease and then block the line.

Grease and soap buildup

Kitchen grease cools and hardens inside pipes. Over time it narrows the line until debris sticks.

Wipes and foreign items

Wipes labeled flushable do not break down fast. Toys and rags can also lodge in bends.

Pipe damage or sags

A belly, offset joint, or cracked pipe holds water and solids. Cables clear the plug but the problem returns.

A line that clears and clogs again within weeks has a cause that the cable did not fix. A camera is how we find it. Young landscaping near a lateral, a rough joint, or a sag all look different on screen.

How a sewer clog gets repaired

Repair starts with clearing the plug and then looking inside. Clearing and fixing are separate steps. Here is how the methods compare.

1

Clear the blockage

A larger machine feeds a cutting cable through the cleanout. For roots and grease, a jetter flushes the pipe wall with high-pressure water.

2

Camera the line

A small camera on a flexible rod shows roots, cracks, offsets, and sags, and records the distance to each.

3

Decide on the fix

Minor findings may need only routine cleaning. A broken or collapsed section needs repair.

4

Repair or replace

Options include digging down to replace the damaged section or relining it from inside. The right one depends on the damage and what sits above the pipe.

Our plumbers describe the options on our sewer line repair page, and if a camera check is all you need, the sewer inspection page explains what you see.

If the fix needs digging

A cracked or collapsed section of sewer pipe sometimes has to be exposed. Idaho law requires a call to 811 (Digline, 1-800-342-1585) at least 2 business days and no more than 10 business days before digging, with the area marked in white paint. That call protects you from hitting gas, electric, and communication lines near the trench.

Plumbing work also needs a permit through the Idaho DOPL, with required inspections on the work. Ask whoever repairs your line how the permit and inspection are handled.

What to ask any quote to include

  • Which method clears the line, cable or jetter, and why
  • Whether a camera inspection is included, and whether you get to see it
  • What happens if the blockage returns soon after
  • How the price changes if the cable has to travel farther or the camera finds damage
  • Who pulls the permit if digging is needed, and how the yard is restored

Cost depends on which line is blocked, access to the cleanout, the clearing method, and whether a repair follows. Insurance may cover water damage from a sudden backup but often not the pipe itself, so ask your insurer. For your real number, call (208) 679-8357. A sewer line replacement is permitted plumbing work under the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho adopts as its State Plumbing Code, and the dig needs a Digline 811 call.
