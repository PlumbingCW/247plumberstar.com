Sewer backup right now: what to do first

A sewer backup is one of the few plumbing problems where acting in the first few minutes changes the damage. Every flush, shower, or load of laundry sends more water into a line that cannot take it.

First steps for a sewer backup

  • ✓Stop using water everywhere: toilets, sinks, showers, washer, and dishwasher.
  • ✓Keep children and pets out of any room with sewage on the floor, and do not wade in it.
  • ✓Do not use chemical drain openers. They sit in a blocked line and are hazardous when the plumber opens it.
  • ✓Photograph the backup, the affected drains, and any damaged belongings for your records.
  • ✓Call us and describe which drains are affected, then follow the dispatcher's instructions.

Sewage carries bacteria, so wear gloves and boots if you must clear belongings, and wash well afterward. If you are unsure whether the area is safe, wait for the plumber.

Signs your main line is the problem

A single clogged sink is a branch problem. A main line problem shows up in the lowest and farthest parts of the house first, because that is where wastewater goes when the line is stopped.

Gurgling toilets

Air pushed back through the traps when water tries to move down a restricted main.

Several drains backing up

Floor drains, tubs, and sinks all slow or overflow at once.

Sewage in the lowest tub or shower

The first place waste surfaces when the main fills up.

House line or public main: how we tell

This question decides who fixes the problem, so we answer it early. In Star, public sewer service comes through the Star Sewer and Water District. If the blockage is in the district's main, you call the district. If it is in your house line, we clear it.

The cleanout test is the first check. We open the cleanout on your line and watch. If the cleanout is dry and clear, the stoppage may be further downstream in the public main. If it fills with wastewater, the blockage is likely on your side. We then confirm with a camera so you are not paying for guesswork.

Homes in Middleton and Eagle should call their city or sewer provider about the public main. Our Middleton and Eagle pages cover those service areas. For where your responsibility ends on your own property, ask the district or your city.

How we clean a main sewer line

1

Reach the line

We use the cleanout if there is one. If there is not, we work from the nearest vent or fixture opening.

2

Break the blockage

A large drum machine with a cutting head opens the stoppage. Roots and heavy debris may need a different head.

3

Scour if needed

Grease, silt, or scale can be washed out with a jetter. See our hydro jetting page for that method.

4

Camera the line

We run a camera to see whether the pipe is clean, cracked, or offset.

If the camera shows damage, clearing the line is only temporary. We explain what we see and, when repair is the answer, our sewer line repair page covers what that involves. A planned sewer camera inspection works the same way when there is no emergency.

Sewer drain cleaning for floor drains and cleanouts

Floor drains in basements and garages connect to the same system and often show the first sign of a backup. Clearing the floor drain without clearing the main only invites the same mess to return. Our drain cleaning page covers the smaller branch lines, while this page covers the main.

Heavy flushing after a storm does not necessarily mean a problem in your line, but a floor drain that fills on a dry day does. If cleaning turns up a pipe that has failed, replacement is governed by the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho adopts as its State Plumbing Code, and we will explain the permit steps first.

What affects the price of sewer line cleaning
