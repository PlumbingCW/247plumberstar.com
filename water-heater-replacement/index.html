A flooded or failed tank: what to do before the plumber arrives

A tank that is leaking from the body, the seam, or the bottom cannot be patched. Water is coming from corroded steel, and the only fix is a new heater. A tank that has simply gone cold is a different story, and it may still be repairable, so check water heater repair first if the tank is dry.

Protect the house while you wait

  • ✓Shut the cold water valve above the tank and turn the handle until it stops.
  • ✓Switch off the breaker for an electric heater, or turn a gas control to off.
  • ✓If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.
  • ✓Open a hot faucet to relieve pressure, and run a hose from the drain valve only if the floor allows it.
  • ✓Take a photo of the data label showing the capacity and the manufacture date.

Choosing a size that fits your household

The most common mistake is buying the same size by habit. A 40-gallon gas tank is a typical swap for a small household, but that is a starting point, not a rule. The right capacity depends on how many people shower, whether you run a large soaking tub, and how much hot water the washer and dishwasher draw at once.

Bigger is not always better. A tank that is too large heats more water than you use and takes more space and fuel to keep warm. A tank that is too small leaves the last person in the house with a cold rinse. If you are weighing a different style, tankless water heater service explains flow rate and sizing.

Matching tank size to the way your home uses hot water

Guests or a home office with a bathroomPeak demand, not average demand

Gas, electric, or heat pump: picking the fuel

Swapping like for like is the simplest path and often the quickest in an emergency. Changing fuel is possible, but it brings new work: a gas line, a vent, or a 240 volt circuit, which is a planned project. That is the territory of water heater installation, and we can do the emergency swap now and plan the conversion later.

Gas tank

Needs a gas supply, a flue, and enough combustion air. Common when the house already has a gas line at the heater.

Electric tank

Needs a suitable circuit and breaker. No flue, so placement is simpler, and replacement is straightforward when the wiring is sound.

Hybrid heat pump

Pulls heat from the air around it. Needs floor space, clearance, a condensate drain, and a room that can spare some cool air.

Options: heat pump (hybrid) water heaters and other upgrades

A heat pump water heater uses an electric compressor to move heat into the tank, so it generally uses far less electricity than a standard electric element heater. It is a real option if you have an electric heater today, the space for it, and a garage or utility room that is not a bedroom wall.

Incentives change from year to year. Ask your electric utility and check current federal and state programs before you buy, because some programs require paperwork before the install. We cannot name amounts or promise eligibility.

  • An expansion tank, if your system is closed and thermal expansion has nowhere to go.
  • A drain pan with a pipe to a safe discharge point, where a leak could damage a floor.
  • New shutoff valves and flexible connectors, so the next service is easier.
  • Seismic or strapping hardware, where it is called for. Ask the plumber what applies.

Venting, combustion air, pan, expansion tank, and relief valve

These details separate a safe replacement from one that merely runs. On a gas heater, the flue must slope and seal correctly so exhaust leaves the building, and the room needs enough air to burn fuel cleanly. If you have a carbon monoxide alarm near the heater, test it after the install.

The relief valve needs a discharge pipe that runs downward to a safe, visible spot, and it must never be capped or blocked. A drain pan is useful anywhere a leak would damage finished floor. An expansion tank belongs on a closed system, and your plumber should check your incoming pressure to see whether one is needed.

Permits and inspections for a water heater swap

The Idaho DOPL says a permit is required when plumbing work is performed. A plumbing contractor can pull the permit for you. A homeowner may pull one only for plumbing work on their own residence and related outbuildings. Required inspections for new plumbing work are ground work, rough-in, and final, and a heater swap may need only the final.

Ask your plumber who pulls the permit and when the inspection will happen. A city building permit may not replace a DOPL permit, so check with Star City Hall at (208) 286-7247 if you are unsure. We also serve Eagle homeowners with the same steps.

How to compare water heater quotes line by line

Two quotes with the same bottom line can cover very different jobs. Ask for each one in writing and match them item by item.

Questions to ask every plumber

  • ✓What brand, capacity, and model is quoted, and what is the manufacturer's warranty?
  • ✓Is the permit and inspection included, and who pulls it?
  • ✓Are new shutoffs, connectors, a relief valve, and a discharge pipe included?
  • ✓Is an expansion tank or drain pan included, or are they extra?
  • ✓What is included in haul-away of the old tank? Ask what is included in the quote.
  • ✓Does the price cover venting and gas line work if the new unit needs different parts?

What shapes the price of a replacement

  • Size and fuelA larger tank or a heat pump unit costs more than a standard gas or electric tank.
  • Venting and gas workA flue that needs rework or a gas line that must be resized adds time and materials.
  • Code itemsPan, expansion tank, relief valve line, and shutoffs may need to be added or corrected.
  • AccessAn attic or tight closet takes longer than an open garage.
  • Permit and haul-awayBoth can be inside the quote or on top of it, so compare them line by line.
