Suspect a leak? What to do first

Most people who call about a leak do not know where it is. They only know something is wrong: water sounds when nothing is running, a musty smell, peeling paint, or a bill that does not match how much water the household used. That is a normal place to start, and it is what a diagnostic visit is for.

Before the plumber arrives

  • ✓Find your main shutoff and learn which way it turns. Close it if water is visibly flowing.
  • ✓Turn off the power to any room where water has reached outlets, switches, or light fixtures.
  • ✓Move furniture, rugs, and electronics away from the wet area.
  • ✓Take dated photos of stains and the meter reading for your insurer.
  • ✓Do not cut into a wall or floor to look. You can lose the clue that shows where the water starts.

Why finding the leak comes before fixing it

Water follows pipes, framing, and slab edges before it shows itself. A ceiling stain can sit several feet from the actual break, so a repair made where the water appears often misses the source. We locate first, then tell you the plan and the price.

If water is actively spraying, burst pipe repair takes over, and a leaking drain line may lead to sewer line repair. We serve Star, Middleton, and Eagle the same way.

The tools we use to pinpoint water without demolition

No single tool finds every leak, so our plumbers combine several based on the pipe and where it runs.

Acoustic listening

Sensitive microphones and ground sensors hear water escaping under pressure. Good for slabs and buried lines.

Electronic leak locators

These instruments filter background noise so we can tell a pipe leak from traffic or a fixture running elsewhere.

Thermal imaging

A hot line leaking under a floor or behind drywall often shows as a warm streak on camera.

Moisture meters

Meters map how far moisture has spread through drywall, wood, and subfloor, so we can trace it back.

Pressure testing

We isolate a section, bring it to test pressure, and watch the gauge. A drop means that section leaks.

Tracer gas

For stubborn leaks, a harmless gas is fed into the line and a sensor follows it to the escape point.

Water meter running or a high water bill: how to check

A bill that climbs with no change in how you live is a common reason people call. The first check costs nothing and takes minutes.

1

Turn everything off

Close every faucet, idle the dishwasher and washer, and keep irrigation off.

2

Open the meter box

Find the meter near the street or property edge. Lift the lid carefully and watch for insects.

3

Read the leak indicator

Most meters have a small triangle or dial that spins with any flow. A moving indicator with the house idle means water is going somewhere.

4

Note the reading and wait

Write down the numbers, wait 30 minutes without using water, and read again. A change confirms a leak.

A moving meter does not say where the leak is. Close your main shutoff. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house, and it could be as small as a toilet silently refilling. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the line before the shutoff, which our water service line repair page covers.

For questions about readings or billing, ask the Star Sewer and Water District, which provides water service to the city and surrounding areas.

Slab leaks: warm floors, running water sounds, and hot water bills

A slab leak is a break in a pipe under or inside a concrete foundation. You rarely see water. You notice a warm spot on a tile or wood floor, the sound of running water with every fixture off, a hot water bill that keeps rising, or a water heater that never seems to rest.

A hot line leaking under concrete warms the slab above it. If one patch of floor feels warmer than the rest, tell the dispatcher.

How we confirm and locate a slab leak

We listen along the floor with acoustic equipment, scan warm areas with a thermal camera, and pressure test the hot and cold lines separately. That usually lets us mark a small area on the floor.

What repair options look like

  • Spot repair: we open the slab at the mark and splice in new pipe.
  • Reroute: we abandon the run under the slab and pipe through walls or the attic.
  • Repipe: if the pipe is corroding throughout, replacing the system beats chasing leaks. See whole house repiping.

Leaks in walls and ceilings

A brown ring on a ceiling, bubbling paint, or a soft drywall seam suggests water behind a surface. The stain is where water ends up, not where it starts. The source can be a supply line, drain joint, shower valve, or tub overflow some distance away. If a ceiling is bulging, set a bucket under it and call.

We map the wet area with a moisture meter and thermal camera, then trace to the fitting. A roof leak or AC condensate line can leave the same stain, and we say so if the evidence points away from pipes.

Under-house and crawlspace leaks

A crawlspace gives us direct access to supply and drain lines. Signs of a leak include a soft floor, warped hardwood, a musty odor through the vents, and damp ground.

We see joints and valves directly and pressure test one branch at a time. Repairs here are usually quicker than in finished walls. Do not enter a wet crawlspace with the power on.

Buried leaks: yard, driveway, and meter-box problems

A buried leak shows up as a soggy lawn patch, a green stripe in dry grass, water pooling in the meter box, or low pressure at every tap. The wet spot can sit some distance from the break.

We use ground sensors and acoustic listening, then confirm with a pressure test. A leak between the meter and the house is a job for water line repair. Meter-box equipment on the utility side belongs to your water provider, so ask the Star Sewer and Water District before touching it.

Call 811 before any digging

Idaho law requires calling 811 (Digline, 1-800-342-1585) at least 2 business days and no more than 10 business days before digging, with the dig area marked in white paint. That applies to a homeowner with a shovel too.

Sprinkler lines, irrigation valves, and pool or hot tub plumbing

Irrigation systems leak at valve boxes, risers, and glued joints. Clues are a wet area that never dries, a weak zone, or a meter that moves while the controller is off. We pressure test a zone and trace the line.

We also locate leaks in plumbing that feeds a hot tub or pool equipment, but we do not repair pool shells.
