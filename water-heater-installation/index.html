What counts as a planned installation

An installation differs from a replacement because something about the setup changes. Maybe you are finishing a basement, adding a bathroom wing, moving the heater out of a bedroom closet, or switching from electric to gas. Each of those changes pipes, venting, or power, so the work is planned in stages.

New construction or remodel

The heater location is set before drywall, so supply lines, drain, vent, and power can be roughed in cleanly.

Relocating a unit

Moving to a garage, closet, or attic means new pipe runs, a new vent path or circuit, and a new place for the drain.

Converting fuel

Electric to gas needs a gas line and a flue. Gas to electric needs a circuit and panel space. Heat pump units need clearance and a condensate drain.

Adding a recirculation line

A return line or pump brings hot water to distant fixtures faster. It adds a return path and a control, and it needs a loop that fits the walls.

Code, permit, and inspection: the steps in order

The Idaho State Plumbing Board adopts the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code as the Idaho State Plumbing Code. DOPL says a permit is required when plumbing work is performed. A plumbing contractor can pull it, and a homeowner may pull one only for plumbing on their own residence and related outbuildings.

1

Plan and permit

We settle the location, fuel, and venting, then the permit is purchased before work starts. Permits expire 365 days after purchase or the last inspection.

2

Ground work and rough-in

On new construction, buried lines and in-wall plumbing are inspected before they are covered.

3

Set and connect

The unit is set, connected, vented, and fitted with a relief valve, discharge pipe, and drain pan where needed.

4

Final inspection

Inspections are requested by phone at 208-334-3950 or online through eTRAKiT. Ask your plumber who requests it and when.

A local city building permit may not replace the DOPL permit, so call Star City Hall at (208) 286-7247 or your city building department to ask what else applies. Unlicensed plumbing work carries civil penalties, and you can check any license at DOPL, (208) 334-3233.

Where the heater goes: closet, garage, attic, or utility room

Placement drives cost and safety. A garage or utility room has the easiest access. A closet needs clearance and, for gas units, enough combustion air. An attic needs a drain pan, a safe path for the discharge pipe, a way to carry the unit up and down, and a floor that can hold the weight of a full tank.

Wherever it goes, think about the next service visit. If someone has to crawl through insulation to reach the shutoffs, the heater is in the wrong spot. Straps or seismic bracing, where required, are a standard item to ask about.

Remodel or new build in the works?

Call early. Where the heater goes affects walls, venting, and gas or electric work, and it is easier to settle before framing closes.

Venting, drain, and pan details we plan up front

  • Gas units need a flue that rises and seals, plus clean air to burn fuel.
  • Power vent and sealed combustion units use fans and different pipe, so the route matters.
  • The relief valve discharge pipe must run downward to a visible, safe termination, and nobody should cap it.
  • A drain pan with a pipe to a floor drain or exterior spot protects finished floors.
  • An expansion tank may be needed on a closed system, depending on your pressure.

If you are thinking about a tankless water heater, plan for venting and gas line size early, since those usually change more than they do with a tank. Our other planned work is covered under plumbing installation.

What to prepare before install day

Clear the path

Move boxes and shelving, and clear a path from the door to the heater location.

Confirm the plan

Agree on location, fuel, size, and what is included in the quote.

Know your shutoff

Locate the main water valve and the breaker or gas shutoff for the heater.

Plan for no water

Water is off for part of the day. Fill a few containers and shower beforehand.

What affects the price of a planned install

  • Scope of changeA new line, a vent run, or a fuel conversion costs more than a like-for-like set.
  • Unit typeTank, heat pump, and tankless units differ in equipment and install work.
  • Access and placementAn attic or crawlspace takes longer than a garage wall.
  • Permit and inspectionsThese are part of the job. Ask whether they are in the quote.
  • Recirculation or extrasReturn lines, pans, expansion tanks, and controls add parts and time.
