What the service line is, and what it is not

A service line is the pressurized pipe between the meter or curb stop and your home. It runs underground, so a leak can go unseen until the ground turns soft.

This is different from a leak inside the house. A leak behind a wall, in a ceiling, or under a floor involves house plumbing, and our leak detection service handles that. A service line is also different from repiping. A whole house repiping job replaces the lines inside your home, not the buried line from the street.

Service line

Buried pipe from the meter or curb stop to the house. Leaks show up in the yard, the meter box, or in pressure.

House plumbing

Supply and drain pipes in walls, ceilings, slab, and crawlspace. Leaks show up as stains, damp spots, and bills.

Repipe

Replacement of the house supply system, often when pipes are corroding or leaking in several places.

Who owns which stretch of the line varies by address, so we do not assume. Ask your water provider. The Star Sewer and Water District provides water service to the city and surrounding areas, and your bill will name your provider.

Low water pressure: when the service line is the cause

Low pressure has many causes. Before you suspect the buried line, check one thing: does it affect every faucet or only one? One weak faucet usually means a clogged aerator or a bad valve at that fixture.

Pressure that drops everywhere, especially together with a wet yard, a moving meter, or a bill spike, points toward a leak or restriction on the service line. A partly closed valve or a failed regulator at the house can cause it too. We check those first because they are quick.

Finding, digging, and replacing the line

We locate the leak with ground-listening gear and a pressure test, so we open one spot instead of trenching the whole yard. Depending on the break, a repair can mean replacing a short section or running a new line. Where site conditions allow, trenchless methods may avoid cutting through a driveway or garden, and we explain what fits your property.

Call 811 before any digging

Idaho law requires calling 811 (Digline, 1-800-342-1585) at least 2 business days and no more than 10 business days before digging, with the dig area marked in white paint. Utility lines run in the same ground as your service line, and we do not dig until the markings are done. Plan for that wait unless the break is an active flood, in which case the main shutoff buys you time.

  • Depth and distanceA short, shallow run is quicker than a long line that crosses the yard.

  • Repair or replaceA single section is a smaller job than replacing the whole line.
  • Digging methodOpen trench and trenchless methods carry different costs and fit different sites.
