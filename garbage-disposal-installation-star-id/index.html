Garbage Disposal Installation in Star, ID: A Step-by-Step Guide

Replacing a garbage disposal is a doable afternoon project if the old unit is already wired and you are swapping like for like. You cut power, remove the old unit from its mounting ring, attach the new mount and hang the disposal, connect the drain and dishwasher line, restore power, and test for leaks. Adding a disposal where none exists is bigger, because it needs a switched outlet and a new drain arrangement.

This guide covers the parts, the order of work, and the mistakes that cause most leaks. If you would rather not do it, our plumbers can take over at any step. Call (208) 679-8357.

Repair, swap, or add: decide before you buy

A disposal that hums but does not spin is usually jammed. Many jams clear with a hex key turned from underneath and a press of the reset button. A disposal that leaks from the body is done, because the seals inside cannot be fixed. A unit that leaks at the sink flange or a pipe joint may only need new gaskets.

If the sink drain itself is slow, fix that before installing anything. A new disposal on a clogged line will back up on day one. Our drain cleaning team can clear the line first.

Parts and tools to gather

Have these on the counter before you start

  • ✓The new disposal, with its mounting hardware and a power cord kit if yours does not include one
  • ✓Plumber's putty or the gasket supplied with the new mount
  • ✓A pair of slip-joint pliers and a screwdriver set
  • ✓A bucket and towels for the trap water
  • ✓A new dishwasher drain hose clamp, if your hose clamp is rusted
  • ✓A flashlight and a work mat or old towel for the cabinet floor

Check the new unit's mounting style against your sink. Most use a quick-lock or three-bolt mount. Look at the discharge outlet position too, since it needs to line up with your trap arm without the pipe being forced.

Install steps, in order

1

Kill the power

Switch off the breaker for the disposal circuit and confirm with a tester at the outlet or the wire box. Unplugging a corded unit is not enough if you will touch bare wires.

2

Disconnect the old unit

Loosen the dishwasher hose and the drain pipe. Support the disposal, then turn the mounting ring to release it, and set it down.

3

Replace the mount

Remove the old flange. Clean the sink opening, set the new flange with putty or a gasket, and tighten until a thin bead of putty squeezes out. Wipe it off.

4

Hang and connect the new unit

Snap or bolt the disposal to the mount. Knock out the dishwasher plug if a dishwasher connects. Attach the discharge pipe and the wiring last, then tighten all joints by hand and a quarter turn.

The dishwasher plug mistake

New disposals have a small plastic plug in the dishwasher inlet. If a dishwasher drains through the disposal, you must knock that plug out with a screwdriver and hammer, and fish the plug piece out of the housing. Skip it and the dishwasher cannot drain, so the water backs up into the dishwasher.

Power: hardwired versus plug-in

Some disposals are hardwired through a junction box and others plug into an outlet under the sink. A plug-in unit needs a cord kit and an outlet on a switched circuit. Matching wire colors is simple when the old connections are clear. It is not simple when a cable has been cut short or the box is damaged.

If the sink has no wiring or no switch, stop. A new circuit or switch is an electrical job, and it involves the same permit questions as plumbing. Hire a licensed pro rather than splicing a cord into a lamp wire.

Permit and inspection questions in Star

Idaho DOPL says a permit is required when any plumbing work is performed, and homeowners may buy a permit only for plumbing work on their own residence. Required inspections are ground work, rough-in, and final. Star City Hall at 10769 W State Street lists plumbing permits on its services page.

For you, that means a disposal swap is not automatically exempt. Call the city at (208) 286-7247 or DOPL at (208) 334-3233 before you start, and ask how a like-for-like replacement is treated. If you hire a plumber, ask who pulls the permit.

Test it before you close the cabinet

  1. Restore power and run cold water for a few seconds.
  2. Flip the switch and listen for smooth grinding, not a grating or humming noise.
  3. Fill the sink and release it to see how the drain moves water.
  4. Run the dishwasher briefly and check the hose connection for drips.
  5. Wipe all joints dry and check again with a paper towel after 10 minutes.

A drip at the flange usually means the putty or gasket is not seated and needs another quarter turn. A drip at a pipe joint often means a misaligned washer. Do not overtighten plastic fittings, since they crack.

When to hand the job to a plumber

Call a plumber if the drain is corroded, the sink flange has damage, a pipe is not aligned, or you are unsure about the wiring. Our plumbers handle garbage disposal repair and replacements, and a bad drain line may need a bigger fix.
