Smell gas? Read this before anything else

Gas safety comes first

If you smell gas, leave the building right away. Do not flip switches, use a phone inside, or light anything. Once you are outside, call the gas utility or 911 BEFORE calling a plumber.

We never promise a gas response time, because the utility and emergency crews handle the emergency. After the area is safe, call us for gas line inspection or installation. Ask the dispatcher whether your particular gas job fits our scope, and they will tell you honestly.

What we install and move

Installation work falls into a few groups, and we sort them on the first call.

Fixtures

Sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers, and utility sinks, set and connected to existing shutoffs and drains.

New lines

Supply and drain runs for an added bathroom, a kitchen island, a laundry room, or a garage sink.

Relocations

Moving a fixture means moving its supply, drain, and vent, which depends on framing and slope.

Remodel rough-in

Pipe set in open walls and floors before drywall and finishes go in, then inspected.

Other related work lives on separate pages. A new heater is covered under water heater installation, and a failing fixture may be a faucet repair instead of a replacement.

Planning a relocation or remodel rough-in

A drain needs slope and a vent. A supply line needs a path to the fixture. Those two facts decide what is easy and what is expensive. Moving a sink a few feet along the same wall is usually simple. Moving a toilet across a room, over a slab, or to a second floor takes more planning.

Bring photos and a rough sketch to the call, and tell us whether the floor is slab, crawlspace, or finished basement. We would rather plan the route before you buy fixtures, because a fixture with a different rough-in size can add work.

New construction plumbing

For new construction, plumbing is built in stages that match the required inspections. Ground work comes first, before the slab or backfill. Rough-in follows, with pipe in walls and floors before they close. A final inspection finishes the job once fixtures are set. We coordinate timing with the builder so inspections do not hold up other trades.

Idaho permits, inspections, and the plumbing code

The Idaho State Plumbing Board, part of the state licensing division, adopts the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code as the Idaho State Plumbing Code. A permit is required when any plumbing work is performed. Required inspections are ground work, rough-in, and final. Permits expire 365 days after purchase or last inspection.

Homeowners may buy a permit only for plumbing work on their own residence and related outbuildings. Plumbing contractors get permits too. A city building permit may not replace the state permit, so check with your city building department as well. Unlicensed plumbing work carries civil penalties, and you can verify any plumber at the state licensing division, (208) 334-3233.

Inspections are requested by phone at 208-334-3950 or online.

Plan the softener loop now, even if you add a softener later

An Idaho amendment to the plumbing code requires a pre-plumbed water softener loop in new one- and two-family homes built slab on grade or with a finished basement. For anyone remodeling or building in that kind of home, the practical tip is simple: put the loop in while the walls and floor are open. Adding one later means cutting into a finished wall or slab.
