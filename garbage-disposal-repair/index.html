Disposal leaking or jammed right now: stay safe first

A disposal has steel parts and a motor wired to household power, often over a wet sink. Never put a hand into the opening, even if the unit looks off. Turn off the wall switch, then flip the breaker.

Before you touch anything

  • ✓Turn off the wall switch and the breaker for the disposal.
  • ✓Unplug the cord, or leave the breaker off if it is hardwired.
  • ✓Empty the cabinet under the sink and set a bucket under any drip.
  • ✓Never reach into the grinding chamber. Use tongs or pliers for anything you can see.
  • ✓Call us if water is running and you cannot tell where it comes from.

Jammed unit: the hex socket and the reset button

A jam happens when something hard wedges the flywheel. The motor strains, hums, and trips its thermal overload. That is the red or black reset button on the bottom of the unit. Wait a few minutes, press it, and see if the unit restarts.

1

Power off

Switch off the wall switch and the breaker. Confirm the unit is dead.

2

Free the flywheel

Many disposals have a hex socket in the center of the underside. Turn it with the wrench that came with the unit, back and forth, until the flywheel moves freely.

3

Remove the cause

Look into the opening with a flashlight and pull out a bone, bottle cap, or fruit pit with tongs.

4

Reset and test

Restore power, press the reset button, run cold water, and switch it on.

If the unit trips again right away, the motor or bearings are failing. Repeated resets shorten the motor's life.

Where disposals leak, and which leaks can be repaired

Dry the unit, run water, and watch which spot gets wet first. Leaks at the sink flange or drain outlet are repairable. A cracked housing or water from the bottom of the body means internal seals have failed, and the unit has to be replaced.

A leak from the dishwasher connection is usually a clamp or the hose. If the port plug was never knocked out at installation, dishwasher water backs up into the sink, and we correct that.

Clogs and smells past the disposal

A disposal grinds food but does not carry grease away. Oil cools in the trap and branch line, and starch builds on top. If both sides of a double sink back up, the clog is down the line. Our drain cleaning service clears it with a cable, and hydro jetting helps when grease keeps returning.

A bad smell comes from a film on the shredder ring and under the rubber splash guard. Run cold water, grind ice with a little dish soap, and scrub the guard.

  • Never grind grease, oil, or fat.
  • Skip coffee grounds, eggshells, pasta, and rice in volume.
  • Keep bones, fruit pits, and fibrous stalks out.
  • Never drop in silverware or bottle caps.

Repair or replace, and what size to buy

Repair makes sense for a loose flange, a bad clamp, a hose fix, or a one-time jam. Replace when the housing leaks, the motor trips again after a reset, or the unit is old enough that parts are not worth chasing.

Disposals come in horsepower sizes. A smaller unit suits light use. A larger motor handles heavier loads and runs quieter under strain. Pick by how often you cook and how much food waste you grind. We can install a unit you bought or supply one. A sink that needs a new fixture too is covered under faucet and sink repair.

Reconnecting a disposal and its dishwasher drain should follow the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho adopts as its State Plumbing Code.
