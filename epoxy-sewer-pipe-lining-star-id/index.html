Epoxy Sewer Pipe Lining in Star: When It Works and When It Does Not

Epoxy sewer pipe lining repairs a damaged drain or sewer line by curing a new pipe inside the old one. A resin-soaked liner is pulled or inflated through the pipe, pressed against the wall, and hardened. It works well for cracks, small root intrusion, and worn joints in a pipe that still holds its shape. It does not fix a collapsed pipe, a bad sag, or a break that has shifted out of line.

The only way to know which side of that line you are on is a camera look inside the pipe. Our sewer inspection team does that and shows you the video. Call (208) 679-8357 if your line is backing up or you want an honest opinion on lining.

How a liner goes in

Lining starts with a clean pipe. Roots, scale, and debris have to come out first, or the liner will not bond. Then a felt or fiberglass tube soaked in epoxy is pulled into place and inflated against the pipe wall until the resin cures.

1

Camera survey

A camera runs the pipe to find cracks, offsets, sags, and how many bends and connections there are.

2

Clean the pipe

Cutters or a hydro jetting pass scour roots and buildup off the wall.

3

Install the liner

The resin tube is drawn in through a cleanout or small access hole and inflated against the old pipe.

4

Cure and reopen

The resin hardens, the ends are trimmed, side connections are reopened, and a final camera pass checks the result.

If your line holds water in a low spot, lining only keeps it there. A sag is a digging job. Our sewer line repair page explains the open-trench option.

What you gain and what you give up

What lining does well

It avoids trenching through a driveway, landscaping, or a finished floor. It closes joints and cracks and can leave a smooth interior.

What it cannot do

It cannot change slope, rebuild a missing section, or rescue a pipe that has lost its shape. The liner also narrows the inside a little.

The liner can cover side connections, so each one must be reopened with a cutter. A poor reinstatement leaves a blocked fixture later. Ask who does that step and how it is checked.

Digging rules and line ownership in Star

Idaho law requires a call to 811 (Digline, 1-800-342-1585) at least 2 business days and no more than 10 business days before digging, with the dig area marked in white paint. Star Sewer and Water District, at 11551 W Tempe Lane, provides sewer service for the city and surrounding developed areas.

That matters for lining in two ways. If lining needs a small access pit, the 811 call applies. And before you pay to repair any pipe, ask the district which part of the line is yours and which belongs to the district, since you should not pay to line a public main.

Source type: Digline and Star Sewer and Water District

The Idaho plumbing board says a permit is required whenever plumbing work is performed. Ask Star City Hall at (208) 286-7247 how a liner is permitted. Homeowners in Middleton and Eagle should call their own city building department.
