When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most clogs are a nuisance. A few are not. If sewage or gray water is coming up through a floor drain, a shower, or a tub, the blockage is probably in the main line, and every minute of use makes the mess bigger.

That is when you call us right away, at any hour. We treat a backup the same way at 2 a.m. as we do at noon, and we answer the line around the clock.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

  • ✓Stop using water everywhere in the house, including the washing machine and dishwasher.
  • ✓Do not flush toilets if water is rising in a tub or shower, because the main line is likely blocked.
  • ✓Move rugs, cardboard, and anything valuable away from the low drain.
  • ✓Skip chemical drain openers. They sit in a blocked pipe and can burn a plumber's skin or ruin a cable head.
  • ✓Take a photo of the problem fixture for your records and tell the dispatcher what you see.

Reading the clog: what slow drains and gurgles are telling you

Where the slowdown shows up tells us where the blockage sits. One slow bathroom sink usually means a hair and soap plug in that fixture's trap or branch line. A gurgle in the tub when the toilet flushes points downstream, to the line they share.

When every fixture is sluggish at once, or the lowest drain in the house is the first to back up, the blockage is in the main line. That is the job that needs a longer cable or a jetter, and it is the one most likely to turn into an emergency.

Customers in Star often describe the same pattern: a drain that clears for a few weeks and then slows again. A repeat clog is rarely bad luck. Something in the pipe is catching the debris, such as a rough joint, a sag, grease buildup, or a root that has found a seam. We would rather find that than clear the same plug three times.

One fixture slow

A cable through the trap and branch line is usually enough. Most of these are finished in a single visit.

Two or more fixtures slow

The blockage is further down the branch. We run a longer cable and check the line with a camera if the cable keeps catching.

Lowest drain backs up

Treat it as a main line problem. Call right away, stop using water, and expect a larger cable or a jetter.

How we snake a drain, step by step

Snaking, or cabling, means feeding a rotating steel cable into the pipe until the cutting head reaches the blockage and breaks it apart or hooks it so it can be pulled out. We match the cable to the line. A small hand-fed cable suits a bathroom sink. A larger machine with a drum of cable is what a four-inch main needs.

1

Find the access

We pick the nearest cleanout, trap, or fixture opening so the cable has the shortest path.

2

Cut and retrieve

The cable head cuts through the plug. We pull out what we can so it does not settle further down.

3

Flush and test

We run water for several minutes to confirm the line carries a full flow, not just a trickle.

4

Camera if needed

If the clog came back before, or the cable snagged, we look inside the pipe to see why.

A cable opens a hole through the blockage. It does not always scrub the pipe wall clean. When grease or heavy scale lines a pipe, a cleared line can narrow again within weeks, and hydro jetting is the better follow-up because water pressure scours the full pipe wall.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, food, and the disposal

A kitchen drain collects grease, coffee grounds, starchy food, and soap film, and all of it cools and hardens inside the pipe. A disposal grinds food finer but does not carry grease away. Over a few months the pipe narrows until a plug forms.

If your sink drains into the disposal and both sides of a double sink are blocked, the clog is past the disposal in the shared line. If the water backs up in the sink when the dishwasher runs, the dishwasher drain tied into the disposal is usually the weak point. A leaking or jammed unit may need its own repair, and our garbage disposal repair page covers that.

When a drain line leaks instead of clogging

Not every drain complaint is a clog. A drain line can crack or separate and leak into a wall or crawlspace, which shows up as a damp stain, a musty smell, or a slow drain that clears and returns. We check for a leak when the cable runs clean but the symptom comes back, and leak detection handles the pinpointing if we suspect water is escaping.
