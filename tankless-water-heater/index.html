What tankless service we do, stated plainly

We flush and descale tankless units, check venting and gas supply, diagnose common error codes, and help size a new unit. Some repairs that involve sealed internal parts or warranty claims may belong to the manufacturer's service network, and we will say so up front.

If another plumber told you they only replace, call around and ask the question directly: do you service tankless heaters, and what does that include? A straight answer saves you a wasted visit.

Why a tankless unit needs periodic service

A tankless heater heats water as it flows through a heat exchanger. Minerals in the water can leave scale on that exchanger, and scale acts like insulation. The unit works harder, throws codes, and can shut down on overheat.

How fast scale builds depends on your water. We do not state hardness for Star or set a service interval for you. Follow the manufacturer's schedule, ask your water provider how hard your water is, or test your tap with a kit.

Signs your unit may need a flush

  • ✓Water runs lukewarm or swings between hot and cold.
  • ✓An error code appears repeatedly, especially one about overheat or flow.
  • ✓The unit sounds noisy, with popping or rumbling.
  • ✓Hot water takes longer to arrive or flow looks weaker.

How a tankless flush and descale works

Most units have isolation valves on the hot and cold lines, sometimes called service valves. They let a plumber close the house supply and connect hoses directly to the unit. If your heater has no isolation valves, adding them is usually the first step.

1

Isolate the unit

Shut off the power or gas and close the house water through the isolation valves.

2

Connect a pump and hoses

A small pump circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger in a closed loop.

3

Circulate the solution

A vinegar-type or manufacturer-approved descaling solution breaks down scale. The time depends on the scale.

4

Rinse and restart

We flush with clean water, clean the inlet filter, reopen the valves, and run the unit to test.

A flush cleans the unit but does not fix a failed part. If the code returns afterward, the next step is a diagnosis. For a leak anywhere around the unit, see water heater repair.

Sizing a tankless unit: flow rate and temperature rise

A tankless heater is rated by how much water it can heat per minute to a given temperature. Two numbers matter. Flow rate is how many gallons per minute you use at once. Temperature rise is how much the water must be warmed, from the incoming cold temperature up to your target.

Add up the fixtures that might run together, such as a shower and a dishwasher. Colder incoming water means more rise and less flow from the same unit. Ask the plumber to size for your coldest months, not the average.

Every tankless unit also has a minimum flow, a low-flow point below which the burner will not fire. That is why a barely open faucet can run cold. If you often draw tiny amounts of hot water, mention it.

Gas line, venting, and other upgrades before you switch

A gas tankless unit pulls much more gas at once than a tank burner. The existing gas line may be too small, and the vent usually needs different pipe and a different route. These are the items that change a swap into a project.

That is where planned water heater installation and permit steps come in. If your tank has failed today, our replacement page explains how to weigh the options. Tankless installs depend on gas sizing and venting rules in the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho adopts as its State Plumbing Code.
