Pump failure with water rising: safety and first moves

Water and electricity do not mix. A pump, its cord, and the outlet can all be live. If water is on the floor near the pit, do not wade in. Turn off the breaker for the pump circuit from a dry spot, and if you cannot reach the panel safely, call us before going near it.

Once the power is off, lift items off the floor. Do not reach into the pit with a bare hand.

While you wait for the plumber

  • ✓Keep out of standing water until the power is off at the breaker.
  • ✓Move boxes, rugs, and electronics above the waterline.
  • ✓Skip laundry and long showers.
  • ✓Check whether the pump is plugged in and the breaker or GFCI has tripped.
  • ✓Call us and describe what the pump is doing.

How to test a sump pump with a bucket of water

A bucket test takes five minutes and shows whether the float, motor, and discharge line work.

1

Confirm power

Make sure the pump is plugged into a working outlet and the GFCI is not tripped.

2

Pour slowly

Add a bucket or two of water into the pit until the float rises.

3

Watch the start

The pump should start by itself, empty the pit, and shut off cleanly.

4

Check the discharge

Listen for water moving through the line and look outside to see it leaving. A heavy knock when it stops means a bad check valve.

If nothing happens, or the pump runs but the water level does not drop, stop and call.

What a failing sump pump is telling you
What you seeLikely causeUsual fix
Pump will not start, pit risingStuck float, dead motor, or no powerFree or replace the float, or replace the pump
Pump runs nonstopFloat stuck up, failed check valve, or oversize flowClear the float, replace the check valve
Pump hums but no water movesJammed impeller or frozen dischargeClean the impeller, clear the line
Loud banging after shutoffCheck valve slammingReplace the check valve
Short cyclingPit too small or float set poorlyReset the float, review the pit

The float switch is the usual weak spot. It can snag on the pit wall or on its own cord, and then the pump either never starts or never stops. The check valve on the discharge line stops water from falling back into the pit. When it fails, the pump cycles on the same water over and over.

Where the water goes: discharge lines and battery backup

The discharge line carries pumped water outside, away from the foundation. Ask your city or sewer provider about where a discharge may go, since not every drain accepts it. Do not tie a sump pump into the house sewer drain without checking first.

A battery backup pump sits beside the main pump or on a separate switch. If a storm cuts power, the backup keeps pumping for a limited time. It does not replace the main pump, and the battery needs periodic checks. Ask us about sizing and testing.

Sewage ejector pump versus sump pump

A sump pump moves groundwater or runoff from a pit. A sewage ejector moves wastewater from a basement bathroom, laundry, or bar sink up to the main drain, and it sits in a sealed basin. A failed ejector is a sanitation problem. If a toilet or a tub in a lower level backs up, stop using that bathroom and call.

We repair and replace both. Our drain cleaning team checks whether a backup is the pump or a blocked line, and a sewer line repair may be needed if the main is the issue.
