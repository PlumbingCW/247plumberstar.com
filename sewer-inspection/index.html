Sewer Camera Inspection and Locating

Sewer Camera Inspection in Star, ID

A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible rod and pushes it through your drain line so you can see the pipe instead of guessing. It is the quickest way to learn whether a backup is a simple clog or a damaged pipe. You get a clear price before the work starts, and the camera shows you the same thing the plumber sees.

When a camera inspection is worth the visit

Not every drain needs a camera. A single slow sink usually does not. But there are situations where a look inside the pipe saves money and guesswork.

Repeat backups

A line that clogs again after cleaning is telling you something. The camera shows what keeps catching debris.

Before buying a home

A buyer can ask a plumber to look at the sewer line before closing. Ask your agent or attorney how that fits your contract.

Before lining or replacement

A repair price based on footage is a firmer number than one based on a guess, and it shows whether lining is even possible.

After roots

Once roots are cut out, a camera shows the opening they used, so you know whether to seal it.

What the camera can show you

A cleanout tied in on its ownA branch or access point joining the mainOften fine, but confirms where the line runs

The camera also shows what is not wrong. A clean line with a steady slope tells you the problem was a one-time blockage, and that is worth knowing before you spend on a repair.

Sewer camera and locate: finding the pipe from above ground

The camera head carries a small transmitter. A plumber walks the yard with a receiver, finds the signal, and marks the surface where the head is. This gives you the position and rough depth of the pipe at that spot.

That matters when something needs to be dug. Marking exactly where a crack sits means the repair can be a small, targeted excavation, not a guess through the yard. Idaho law still requires calling Digline at 811 (1-800-342-1585) at least 2 business days and no more than 10 business days before digging, so the surface marks guide the plumber, while 811 covers the utilities.

How a camera visit goes

1

Find the access

We use your cleanout when there is one, or a vent or fixture opening when there is not.

2

The camera feeds down the line while you or the plumber watch the screen.

3

Mark and record

Problems are noted by distance from the entry point, and locating marks go on the surface when needed.

4

Walk through it

We show you the findings and say whether the line needs cleaning, repair, or nothing.

Recording and what you receive

Camera equipment commonly records video, and many systems can save stills of problem spots. What you receive depends on the equipment and the visit. Ask what the report includes before the camera goes in, and ask whether you can keep the footage if you plan to compare repair estimates or share it with a buyer, seller, or insurer.

What a camera cannot do

A camera cannot see through a full blockage, so a completely stopped line may need cleaning first. Sometimes that means a cable, or hydro jetting after the camera shows the pipe can take it. Our sewer line cleaning page covers the cleaning side.

It also shows the inside of the pipe, not the soil around it, and it may not travel past a collapsed section or a sharp bend. If the camera stops, we tell you where and why.

Whose pipe is it? In Star, sewer service is through the Star Sewer and Water District, so if the camera shows trouble out in the public main, you call the district. For exactly where your responsibility ends, ask the district. Our Middleton and Eagle pages cover those areas. The camera report is most useful when a repair follows, and that repair would be governed by the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Idaho adopts as its State Plumbing Code.

What affects the price of a camera inspection

  • Access to the lineA cleanout makes it quick. Pulling a toilet to get in takes more time.
  • Length of the runA long line, or one that branches, takes longer to cover.
  • Cleaning firstA line that is partly blocked may need clearing before the camera passes.
  • LocatingMarking the pipe depth and position on the surface adds time.
  • RecordsSaved footage or a written summary can change the scope, so ask what is included.

Insurance often does not pay for an inspection on its own, but a covered backup may involve one. Ask your insurer. For your real number, call (208) 679-8357.
